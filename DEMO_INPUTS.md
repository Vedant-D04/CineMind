# CineMind 2.0 demo inputs

## 1. Recorded tennis footage

Choose **Film & scenes** and **Meta live**. Use a 15–30 second excerpt if possible; current upload limits are 60 seconds / 100 MB. Preserve its real aspect ratio. This brief does not assume a camera angle or lighting condition we have not seen.

### The scene or script excerpt

I am practicing tennis alone, hitting a ball against a wall. Present this as a grounded training sequence in a short sports film. Assess how clearly the existing footage shows the player, racket, practice area and relationship to the wall.

### What should the audience feel?

Focus, persistence and the solitude of practicing alone. Keep the training action readable and visually intentional rather than making it look like an accidental phone recording.

### What do you have to work with?

Already-recorded phone footage of one player and a wall, using the light and location visible in the clip. Preserve the original aspect ratio. Separate feasible editing or grading changes from improvements requiring a reshoot. This is filmmaking feedback, not tennis technique coaching. A ball may be too small or absent in a sampled frame; state uncertainty rather than inventing its position, trajectory or timing.

### Actions

1. Choose **Review a take**, fill the scene brief, then **Continue to video**; no generated shot is needed.
2. Upload your actual tennis clip under **Upload recorded video**, not Bring an influence.
3. Press **Get director’s notes**.
4. Read one actual observation before narrating it. Pick a timestamp, then Locate in frame when the report provides a region.
5. Select a different thumbnail; the player should pause and seek to it.
6. Export footage insights. Keep the screen recording before switching to Plan & shoot, which clears the clip. Editing the intention retains it but invalidates the old report.

### What to check

Four timestamps should have six craft checks each: subject visibility, image clarity, composition, background/edges, light/exposure and scene/intention. Useful findings might involve cropped limbs/racket, insufficient action space, distracting edges, or weak subject separation—but these are possibilities, not promised findings about footage we have not seen. Clear and uncertain results are valid. Four stills cannot establish stroke quality, ball tracking, stabilization, cut timing, pacing or audio.

## 2. Live iPhone camera on the Mac

Keep the existing iPhone-as-camera connection. The Mac runs the UI. Set this up before recording; show a brief inset or external shot of the phone to establish which camera is supplying the feed.

### The scene or script excerpt

A student sits at a desk working through a difficult problem on a laptop, then looks down to make a note. This is a quiet, observational moment in a short film.

### What should the audience feel?

Quiet concentration with a little pressure. The student and desk action should be readable, with enough room around them for the setting to matter.

### What do you have to work with?

One performer, an iPhone used as the Mac's camera, a laptop and the available room light. Work with the actual daylight or room lighting; do not force a night look. Assess all visible craft issues, including subject cropping and background distractions, alongside lighting.

### Chosen shot: edit Your framing after choosing an option

Medium, eye-level shot from roughly the waist or desk up. Keep the full head, looking room and hands or working area visible. Hold the camera steady and leave some surrounding space. The subject is working rather than addressing the lens.

### Live demonstration

1. Start from Describe your scene, paste this live brief and explore shot options in Meta mode.
2. Choose the option closest to a medium shot; generated titles/order vary. Edit Your framing to the text above if necessary.
3. Open camera and select the iPhone from the Camera selector when available. Allow the normal browser permission yourself.
4. Begin with slightly too little headroom, ensuring the framing is visibly different from the chosen brief. Start live guidance and wait for its actual response.
5. If it identifies cropping, widen/tilt the frame to include your head. If it identifies another visible issue, discuss that real result instead of pretending it flagged cropping.
6. Wait for the next reply; show whether the issue is resolved, persists or is uncertain. Do not promise a particular model judgment.
7. Inspect analyzed snapshot, select a real callout if available, then pause guidance and stop camera.

The first snapshot is sent immediately. Later snapshots are captured 12 seconds after each successful reply; response time adds to the interval. Eight attempted requests end a session. Keep the tab visible. Callouts belong to analyzed snapshots, not continuously tracked video.
